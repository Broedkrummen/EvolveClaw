# Initial literature sprint: evaluating EvolveClaw's next phase

Date: 2026-10-13. This is a bounded initial evidence brief, not an exhaustive literature review or an implementation plan. Firecrawl was used for discovery and primary-paper passages; raw outputs and failed retrievals are retained under `literature-sources/`. No code, live configuration, or paid model evaluations were changed or run.

## Findings that should govern evaluation

**Evaluate whether remembered experience improves later actions, not only whether the assistant can repeat a remembered fact.** The strongest directly relevant evidence is MemoryArena's separation between recall-oriented memory scores and sequential task completion. For EvolveClaw, an injection hook executing successfully proves delivery of guidance; it does not establish that the guidance is correct, remains current, improves actions, or is safe to persist.

**Treat obsolete-memory use as a distinct failure even when an answer also contains the right new fact.** LongMemEval's knowledge-update judge explicitly accepts an updated answer that also includes previous information. That is reasonable for some recall questions, but cannot establish correct invalidation, deletion, or downstream behavior. STALE directly tests the gap between retrieving an update, resisting stale premises, and applying the update in a later recommendation.

**Persistent experience is an attack surface, including experiences generated by the assistant itself.** MINJA demonstrates query-induced stored malicious reasoning. MemoryGraft demonstrates poisoning through ingestion artifacts that resemble successful procedures. These papers justify testing provenance and retention boundaries; they do not establish that adding a scope label, classifier, or rollback button reliably prevents poisoning.

## Verified core sources and exact limits

### LongMemEval: updated facts and abstention

Primary [paper, arXiv 2410.10813v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813v2); [author repository](https://github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval); [released dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/xiaowu0162/longmemeval-cleaned).

Evidence level: **primary in-body passages**, plus author-repository search excerpts; repository code was not successfully scraped or independently executed.

The benchmark has 500 questions covering extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstention. Standard histories are about 115K tokens (S) or 500 sessions/about 1.5M tokens (M). QA is judged with `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`; retrieval is measured with Recall@k and NDCG@k. Judge validation uses 30 examples per question type and two answering models, with approximately 97–98% average agreement; this is not universal evaluator accuracy. The commercial chatbot study uses only 97 questions, 3–6 sessions, excludes abstention and some other types, and dates to August 2024.

Important scoring limit: the knowledge-update rubric accepts previous information alongside the updated answer. Keep update correctness and obsolete-memory dependence as separate measurements. These are memory QA outcomes, not verified tool/task completion.

### LoCoMo: long conversation recall, with a version caveat

Primary [arXiv 2402.17753v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17753v1), dated 2024-02-27; [author project](https://snap-research.github.io/locomo/); [author repository](https://github.com/snap-research/LoCoMo); [ACL final paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.747.pdf).

Evidence level: **primary in-body passages from v1 and successfully scraped author repository**; the ACL final revision was located through primary search excerpts and was not fully reviewed in this sprint.

The inspected v1 reports 50 conversations, 19.3 sessions on average, 9,209.2 average tokens, and 7,512 QA items: single-hop 2,705; multi-hop 1,104; temporal 1,547; open-domain 285; adversarial 1,871. Conversation generation is grounded in personas/event graphs, followed by human correction. It also studies event summarization and multimodal dialogue generation. Adversarial here includes unanswerable conversation questions; do not equate that category with a persistent-memory security test.

The ACL final primary search excerpt reports different conversation statistics: 588.2 turns, 27.2 sessions, 16,618.1 tokens. The scraped author repository explicitly states its current release consists of **ten conversations**, in `data/locomo10.json`, selected from the original 50 for longest conversations, annotation quality, and cost-effective evaluation. It does not release the images themselves. Therefore identify the exact paper/dataset version and QA subset before repeating a score. QA scoring implementation was not fully inspected; no numeric cross-vendor ranking is endorsed.

### MemoryBank: a small simulated companion study

Primary [arXiv 2305.10250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10250), titled *MemoryBank: Enhancing Large Language Models with Long-Term Memory*. The retrieved literature lists its AAAI 2024 publication, but that conference record was not independently fetched in this sprint.

Evidence level: **primary in-body passages**, although the retrieved index text does not expose an exact revision header; no latest-version claim is made.

Quantitative evaluation uses ten days of conversations from **15 virtual users simulated by ChatGPT** and 194 probing questions. Qualitative examples involve real-user dialogue. SiliconFriend also incorporates psychological-dialogue tuning, so better companion responses do not isolate a memory-only intervention. The method combines stored conversations, event summaries, user portraits, retrieval, and time/repetition-based forgetting inspired by the Ebbinghaus curve.

This supports testing retention/decay as a design choice. It does not establish that all important facts should decay, that a user's deletion request reliably removes every derived copy, or that remembered psychological portraits are accurate. This modest simulated recall study cannot be generalized into proven everyday-assistant task completion, provenance safety, or rollback efficacy.

### MemoryArena: memory that changes task execution

Primary [arXiv 2602.16313v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16313v1), dated 2026-02-18; [author project and dataset entry point](https://memoryarena.github.io/).

Evidence level: **primary in-body passages**, including dataset tables; code/evaluators were not run.

Tasks contain causally interdependent subtasks across sessions. The benchmark covers bundled shopping (150 task groups), group travel planning (270), progressive search (256), mathematics (40), and physics (20). Reported overall horizon averages 57 actions; histories exceed 40K tokens on average, with substantial per-domain variation. Later actions require earlier constraints/feedback rather than merely being independent tasks presented sequentially.

The authors report that agents strong on LoCoMo perform poorly in this setting. This supports including actual completion of dependent workflows. It does not establish a universal correlation coefficient, nor prove the superiority of one memory architecture. Detailed per-domain scoring code was not inspected; no unsupported aggregate improvement percentage is quoted.

### STALE: implicit changes in everyday user state

Primary [arXiv 2605.06527, canonical entry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527); [v1 HTML located by primary search](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06527v1).

Evidence level: **primary in-body passages**; the paper-index passages do not independently pin a latest revision date, so avoid silently treating them as a verified latest-version reproduction.

STALE has 400 expert-validated scenarios and 1,200 queries across over 100 everyday topics, with contexts up to 150K tokens. It separates State Resolution, Premise Resistance, and Implicit Policy Adaptation. Automatic Boolean judging receives old state, updated state, hidden invalidation logic, and three responses. Its inspected abstract reports best evaluated overall accuracy of 55.2%, under that study's settings.

This is especially relevant to outdated travel preferences, changed plans, and consequences of changed circumstances. It is a controlled diagnostic benchmark of **one-shot implicit transitions**; its limitations identify multi-step cascades/coupled updates/schema-free scenarios as future work. It does not prove lifelong state maintenance, secure deletion, or rollback.

### MINJA: poison learned through normal-looking interaction

Primary [arXiv 2503.03704v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03704v5); [NeurIPS 2025 conference paper entry](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118152).

Evidence level: **primary in-body passages and conference source**. This is a published attack study, not an EvolveClaw penetration test.

The attacker can query the agent and observe outputs rather than directly editing its memory. Bridging reasoning and progressively shortened indications cause the agent to generate stored malicious experiences used by later victim queries. The paper reports 98.2% average injection success and 76.8% average attack success across three specific agents/task settings. These metrics differ: storing poison is not the same as later harmful behavior. The settings include EHR reasoning, web action planning, and QA, with engineered victim/target terms.

Targeted detection prompts do not generalize across all agents; general prompts can cause false positives. Neither reported rate is a universal rate for personal assistants. Shared-memory/retrieval assumptions and model utility matter. Memory scoping and filtering remain proposals to evaluate, not verified comprehensive defenses.

### MemoryGraft: procedural poisoning, with a crucial metric correction

Primary [arXiv 2512.16962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16962); [v1 fulltext entry](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16962v1).

Evidence level: **primary in-body passages**. The inspected paper focuses on MetaGPT's single DataInterpreter agent and semitransparent ingestion surfaces.

The experiment uses 100 benign seeds, 10 poisoned procedure seeds, and 12 handcrafted queries. The primary quantitative result is **Poisoned Retrieval Proportion: 23/48 = 47.9%**. That is retrieval exposure, not a measured 47.9% harmful-task completion rate. The paper discusses persistent procedural drift and poisoning through README-style ingestion artifacts, but acknowledges aggregate retrieval focus, small handcrafted data, and white/semi-white-box knowledge of what the agent ingests.

Secondary snippets asserting below-0.1% poisoning and above-80% attack success are not supported by the inspected primary experiment and should not be carried into an EvolveClaw assessment. Claims that this paper proves broad cross-agent propagation, complete behavioral effectiveness, or working rollback are also unsupported.

## Evaluator axes proposed for EvolveClaw

These are recommendations inferred from the evidence, not already established capabilities of the plugin.

| Axis | Concrete everyday-assistant probe | Measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dependent task completion | Planning sessions establish budget/transport constraints, then create a later itinerary or shopping draft. | Objective constraint satisfaction and completed steps; keep recall QA separate. |
| Current facts and supersession | User changes preference, location, availability, or project convention; later task contains an old premise. | Correct new-state use, stale-state use, and unwarranted overwrite as separate rates. |
| Implicit change | New circumstances indirectly invalidate earlier advice. | Resolution, resistance to old-premise questions, and actual adapted plan. |
| Unknown/conflicting information | Evidence is absent or equally credible sources disagree. | Correct abstention/clarification versus unsupported certainty; selective accuracy and answer coverage. |
| Provenance | Same statement comes from direct user instruction, model speculation, or retrieved/tool text. | Source attribution accuracy, retention decision, evidence traceability, and unsupported generalization. |
| Scope | Reuse similar topics across independent users, projects, agents, or tasks. | Unrelated-scope retrieval/injection rate and shared-rule utility; test enforced identities, not only labels. |
| Forgetting | Correct/delete a fact or retire a misleading procedure; restart and retrieve again. | Residual usable copies, obsolete-rule application, and accidental loss of unrelated useful rules. |
| Poisoning persistence | A controlled document/tool response suggests a bad shortcut; execute clean later sessions. | Poison admission, retrieval exposure, unsafe action adoption, sessions until recovery; distinguish all four. |
| Utility cost | Same frozen tasks/model with memory off and on. | Completion, latency, prompt tokens, synthesis requests/cost, and user corrections. |
| Stability | Repeat equivalent paraphrases and benign task drift over many sessions. | Outcome variance, rule churn, conflicting rules, irrelevant-injection rate, and degradation over time. |

Use frozen model versions, identical tool environments, token budgets, and paired task histories. Compare memory disabled, strategic only, tactical only, and both; add an equal-compute/equal-token baseline where practical so extra reflection calls are not mistaken for a memory benefit. Separate development histories from held-out task families. Report failures and uncertainty intervals rather than a lone mean score. Audit LLM-judged samples against independent human/objective labels, and keep judges blind to which condition produced the result.

Rollback is a recovery hypothesis. Test whether restoring a snapshot removes poisoned derived entries as well as the original, survives restart, and preserves unrelated newer memory. It cannot undo already executed external actions. Scoping is likewise an enforcement hypothesis: verify reads, writes, synthesis, consolidation, and injection boundaries, and account for intentionally shared rules. Neither a storage snapshot nor an agent-name namespace alone establishes those properties.

## Search and evidence limitations

Discovery surfaced LongMemEval-V2 (arXiv 2605.12493), Memora/From Recall to Forgetting (2604.20006), MemOps (2607.12893), and TANGLE/irreducible-conflict evaluation (2608.13921). Their primary titles/entries were found, but methods were not sufficiently verified in this sprint to recommend implementations or repeat new performance claims. They are follow-up candidates rather than established evidence in this memo.

Canonical scraping repeatedly returned `INVALID_ARGUMENT` or internal errors; a attempted LoCoMo `v2` HTML returned a genuine missing-document page. The primary paper-index passages succeeded. Those passages are selective, can retrieve references as well as methods, and do not amount to full-paper/code review. Failed raw outputs remain saved so coverage is transparent. Shared Firecrawl throughput constrained later calls; after coordination, new calls were capped at two per minute for this agent. No product-marketing comparison was used as primary efficacy evidence. No broad AI-security survey or paid benchmark run was attempted.
